### 2. Hack the Slush Experience

**By:** Slush (slush.org)
**Link:** https://hackjunction.app/participate/53/challenges/248

**The challenge**

Build a tool that makes Slush more valuable for the founders and investors who attend it.

Every year, thousands of founders and investors come to Helsinki for two days of Slush. Everyone arrives with the same goal: meet the right people, have the conversations that matter, and leave with momentum. But with a packed venue, a dense program, and thousands of attendees competing for the same hours, it is easy to spend the event in the wrong queue, the wrong room, or the wrong meeting. The best connection of the week might be twenty meters away, and you'd never know.

Your mission is to build something that founders, investors, or both can actually use at Slush for a better experience. This could mean smarter matchmaking between founders and the right investors, tools for preparing for or following up on meetings, ways to navigate the program and venue, better ways to capture and act on what happens in hundreds of short conversations, or something entirely new. Pick a real user, understand their pain, and build a working prototype that someone could pick up and use at the next Slush.

**Insight — they are especially excited about**

- Solutions that start from a sharp understanding of a specific user: a first-time founder, an angel investor, a partner at a large fund, a founder raising a Series A
- Better matchmaking. Helping the right founders and investors find each other, beyond keyword filters
- Tools that make the time before and after Slush more valuable, not just the two days themselves
- Turning the chaos of hundreds of short meetings into something structured and actionable
- Thoughtful use of AI that respects attendees' privacy and time
- Prototypes that are realistic to pilot at an actual Slush
- Surprising them with something they haven't thought of

**Resources**

Mentors from the Slush team will be available throughout the weekend to share how the event works behind the scenes, what attendees struggle with, and what has been tried before.

You are free to use any tools, frameworks, or platforms you like.

**Judging criteria**

None listed on the challenge page.

**Prizes**

Hackathon Overall Winner Prize: 1000€

### 3. Prodeko.org: New Website & Migration Plan

**By:** Tuotantotalouden kilta Prodeko (https://www.prodeko.org)
**Link:** https://hackjunction.app/participate/53/challenges/245

**Background**

Prodeko.org's current content is partly outdated. The hackathon aims to design a modern, easy-to-maintain new website.

**The challenge**

Design a modern, visually strong website that is easy to update, respects historical content, and is realistically achievable, including a migration plan from the current site.

**Deliverables**

- Homepage & key page concepts
- Sitemap of the new site
- Migration plan from the current prodeko.org
- Platform choice with rationale
- Bonus: a clickable prototype or working MVP

**Constraints**

- No historical content may be lost
- Alumni registry (matrikkeli) kept separate
- Solution must be cost-conscious
- Content must be editable by non-developers

**After the hackathon**

The best proposal becomes the starting point for Prodeko.org's actual renewal project. Goal: a credible vision and a concrete migration path, not a finished production system.

**Insight — six areas the team should map out**

1. Content inventory: map current pages, images & downloads; mark what's kept, updated or removed
2. Matrikkeli (alumni registry): separate service or lightweight app, not merged with the member registry
3. Data stores: page images, officer photos, Proleko magazines, historical board/officer data, and how each migrates
4. Forms: contact, PTER & others may initially use external Forms tools
5. Access rights & SSO: own-SSO feasibility, access model, Enterprise licence costs
6. Platform comparison: see table below
